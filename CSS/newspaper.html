NEW DELHI: The Supreme Court on Thursday underscored the urgent need for structural reforms in the National Testing Agency (NTA), observing that mechanical changes and routine reshuffles have failed to address the deep-rooted issues plaguing the examination body.

“It should not be that a committee gives recommendations, then a new committee is formed which removes the old one, lock, stock and barrel,” Justice P.S. Narasimha said.

The court said reforms must be vibrant, institutionalised, and carried on within the NTA by successive officers.

THE CASE

Several petitions have been filed in the top court over alleged irregularities in examinations conducted by the NTA.

The court pointed out the cycle of reforms which are implemented in one exam, after which senior NTA officers are shifted out and the reforms are undone in the next exam.

There ought to be meaningful continuity in resolving systemic problems within the NTA, the court said.

The bench also pointed out to how the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) has been functioning with relative stability and credibility over the years.

KEY TAKEAWAY

The Supreme Court stressed on long-term, institutional reforms over short-term fixes to restore credibility in the NTA ecosystem.
